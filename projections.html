Projected scores — and how good they are not
Read this before using these to bet. Measured walk-forward on 3,108 games from 2023–2025, the Fathom projected margin misses by 13.95 points on average. The market spread on the same games misses by 11.89. We are 2.06 points worse (p < 10⁻⁴⁰). Picking over/under off our projected total went 50.6% — a coin flip. These scores are published because a number like “31–24” is easier to read than “+7.0”, not because they beat the number you can already get from a book.

Where the score comes from

The team rating solves for one number per team against margin. This solves for two — an offence and a defence rating — against points scored, so a score falls out directly instead of only a margin. The two agree closely (r = 0.981 with the published Fathom margin), which is the point: it is the same opinion, written so you can read it.

Method

Points scored are fit as league mean + offence(team) + defence(opponent) + home field, iterated to convergence, with each team anchored to its own offence and defence ratings from last season and that anchor fading as games accumulate — the same prior, with the same weight, as the team and player ratings. Margin is not capped here: capping points would distort the scoring level, which is the quantity being estimated.

No betting line is used to produce these numbers. When a game's result lands, the projection is graded against the closing spread, and the grade published is the against-the-spread result — not whether the projected winner won.

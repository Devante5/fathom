Why the comparison is to those rules and not to a rival model

A forecast is only worth something if it beats what you could do without it. Backing the home team costs nothing. Ranking teams by their average point differential costs nothing. A model that cannot clear those is decoration. Every number above is measured against them on the same held-out games, and the comparison is reported whether it flatters the model or not — which is why projected totals are missing from the other tab.

Method

One rating per team explains margin. Splitting that same fixed point in two — an offence rating and a defence rating solved against points scored — produces a projected score directly. Each team is anchored to its own prior-season rating with the anchor fading as games accumulate, so an early-season number is not built on two games pretending to be a season.

No betting lines are used anywhere in the projections themselves; the market column is shown for comparison only, after the fact. This is a description of what the model expects, not betting advice, and a 0.52-point gap to the closing line is nowhere near an edge over it.

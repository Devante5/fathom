Player Ratings — production, measured against the defences actually faced

What the number means

FPP is Fathom Player Points per game: what a player's production has been worth in points, after subtracting what the defences he faced give up to everyone else. FPS — the Fathom Point Scale — places that value on a fixed 0–10 scale against every rated player at his own position since 2022: 5.0 is the median starter, 8.0 the 90th percentile, 10.0 the edge of the record. It is the same scale the team ratings use, read within a position rather than across all of FBS.

Compare quarterbacks to quarterbacks. A completed pass is credited in full to the thrower and in full to the catcher, so these numbers are honest within a position and meaningless added across a roster.

Method

The team rating solves for the numbers that best explain every margin once schedule strength is removed. Run that same solver on yards and touchdowns instead of margin and it returns, for every defence, how much it gives up above or below par — adjusted for the offences it has faced. Subtract a defence's figure from a player's line, in proportion to his share of his team's production that day, and what is left is measured against a common opponent. Yards and touchdowns are then converted to points by weights fit from four seasons of team results, not borrowed from a fantasy table.

A player with three games has told us little, so every rating is anchored to his rate from last season, with the anchor fading as games accumulate. A player with no last season starts at replacement level rather than at average, marked NEW in the table — he has to play his way up rather than being handed a number he has not earned.

None of this was assumed. On 38,570 held-out player-weeks across 2023–2025, predicting each player's next game: the raw per-game average missed by 3.94 points, the opponent adjustment cut that to 3.85 (p < 10⁻²¹), and the anchor to 3.80 (p < 10⁻⁴²). Both held in every season and at every position taken separately. Replacement-level priors beat average priors for newcomers (3.37 vs 3.45, p < 10⁻²²).

No betting lines are used anywhere in this rating, and it is not a betting model — our own prop models were measured against real posted lines and lost to the market. This is a description of what has happened. Games played is shown for every player; a rating built on two games deserves less of your confidence than one built on ten.

Player Projections — week 7, projected opportunities

Why opportunities, and not yards

A stat line is two things multiplied together: how many chances a player gets, and what he does with each one. We measured both across four seasons. A player's own prior efficiency predicts his next game's efficiency worse than simply using the league average — R² of −0.08 for receiving and −0.10 for passing. His prior opportunity volume predicts the next game's volume at +0.23 to +0.32.

So this page projects the half that can be forecast and leaves the half that cannot. Projecting receiving yards would multiply a real signal by a random one and present the result as a forecast. Carries, targets-per-se and pass attempts are the honest quantities, and they are also the ones that decide a stat line.

Sportsbooks post these same three quantities under different names: Carries is listed as rushing attempts, Pass Att as passing attempts, Rec as receptions. Same number, different label — worth knowing if you are reading this beside a posted line.

Why the three are produced differently

Each of the three was tested the same way: trained on earlier weeks only, scored on later ones, and compared against the same rows using the player's own prior average as the control. They did not come out the same, so they are not presented the same.

Pass attempts — the model wins by 5.7% and wins in all four usage quartiles, by the most among the lowest-volume quarterbacks. How often a quarterback throws genuinely depends on the game he is in, and the model reads that.

Carries — the model's advantage is almost entirely bell-cow backs. Above 16 carries a game it beats the average by 0.81; between 5 and 7 it is worse, by 0.09. Publishing one number for both regimes would be publishing the worse number to most of the table, so the model is used above nine carries a game and the player's own average below it.

Receptions — the model beat the average by 0.008 of a reception, with 87% of its weight resting on that same average. That is not a model, it is an average with extra steps, so the average is what is printed.

These are projections of opportunity, not of outcome, and not betting advice. No betting line is used anywhere in producing them. A projection built on one completed game deserves far less of your confidence than one built on four — games played is shown for every player, and 23% of these rest on a single game.
